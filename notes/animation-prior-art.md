# Animation API — prior-art comparison

How our landed spec (`animation-primitives-spec.md`) stacks up against the signal/reactive
animation libraries that faced the same problem: Svelte (`spring`/`Tween` runes), Reanimated
(`useSharedValue` + `withSpring`/`withDecay`/`useDerivedValue`), Motion/Framer (`MotionValue`/
`useSpring`/`useTransform`/`useVelocity`), react-spring, VueUse + Motion-for-Vue, Solid
(`@solid-primitives`, solid-motionone), Angular animations. 7-library web-verified survey → comparison.

**Net:** we landed on the *correct* side of every contested decision, with four genuine improvements
over prior art — and two release-blockers + one decision to re-litigate (see end).

**Key architectural validation:** the React/Vue ecosystem builds a *parallel* signal system
(`MotionValue`) only because their native reactivity is too coarse for per-frame updates ("changes
update the DOM without triggering a Vue re-render"). Solid's fine-grained signals give us that for
free — so `createSpring` returns a real `Accessor` and we skip the entire `MotionValue` /
`useTransform` / `useMotionValueEvent` machinery. solid-motionone only adopted the `<Motion>` wrapper
because plain Solid lacked a DOM-bound animated value; our renderer's fine-grained FuseNode rebuilds
mean we don't share that constraint — which is exactly why rejecting `<View animate>` was right.

---

## Consensus & where we land

### value-is-a-signal
**Field:** Near-universal convergence. Svelte 5 (`.current`/`.target` are `$state`), Motion (MotionValue, an explicit "signal-like value"), react-spring (`SpringValue extends FrameValue`, a free-standing observable), Reanimated (`useSharedValue`), Solid primitives (`createTween`/`createSpring` return `Accessor`/tuple), VueUse `useTransition` (returns a `ComputedRef`). The animated value IS a reactive cell you read, not a component. Only Angular's legacy DSL and the declarative wrappers (solid-motionone `<Motion>`, `@vueuse/motion` `v-motion`) reject this — and the rewrite of Angular (`animate.enter`) plus the field's general drift shows the component-wrapper model losing.

**Us:** ✓ **Validated, and idiomatically correct.** `createSpring -> [value, set]` deliberately mirrors `createSignal`'s tuple — the exact same move @solid-primitives/spring made. `createElapsed() -> Accessor` and `createVelocity(signal) -> Accessor` keep everything in the signal graph. You explicitly **REJECTED** the `<View animate>` wrapper, which is the right call: it's the model Angular is migrating *away* from and that solid-motionone only adopted because plain Solid had no DOM-bound animated-value concept — a constraint you don't share since your renderer already does fine-grained FuseNode rebuilds.

### target-vs-from / entrance
**Field:** Two camps. The value-primitive camp is **target-only** with no explicit "from" (Svelte `new Spring(0)`, Motion `x.set()`, Reanimated `withTiming(to)`, Solid `createSpring`, VueUse `useTransition`) — entrance is "construct at start, then retarget in an effect." The declarative camp has first-class from→to: react-spring `{ from, to }`, solid-motionone `initial`/`animate`, `@vueuse/motion` `:initial`→`:enter`, Angular `void => *`.

**Us:** ✓ **Validated, and you took the better half of both.** `createSpring(target, { from? })` — target-only by default (matches the primitive camp) but with an optional `from` for declarative entrance (the one genuinely useful idea from the declarative camp). react-spring is your closest precedent here: `from` as an optional entrance start, current value as the implicit from otherwise. Making `from` a per-transition (constructor-only = entrance, non-persistent) field is sharper than react-spring's `reset:true` replay semantics and avoids the solid-motionone `initial={false}` special-case.

### follow-a-reactive-value
**Field:** **Strong consensus this is a flagship feature**, with a clean split on whether it's automatic. Automatic/declarative: VueUse `useTransition(() => derived())` (its *entire purpose*), Svelte `Spring.of(() => expr)`, Solid `createDerivedSpring(accessor)` (literally `createSpring` + `createEffect(() => set(target()))`), Motion `useSpring(sourceMotionValue)`, react-spring's render-reactive object form. **Explicitly NOT automatic:** Reanimated — `toValue` is snapshotted at call time, following requires an explicit `useAnimatedReaction`. The whole field treats Reanimated's snapshot model as the deliberate-but-notable outlier.

**Us:** ✓ **Validated, best-in-class.** `target = a value OR ()=>signal` where the thunk form is **derived-writable: follows the signal AND is settable**. This is strictly more powerful than the field: Svelte's `Spring.of` and Solid's `createDerivedSpring` are read-only when given a thunk — you can't also `set()` them. You collapsed the two modes (`createSpring` static + `createDerivedSpring` follow) into one overloaded `target` param. That's the right unification, and the writable-follower is a genuine improvement nobody else ships cleanly.

### interruption + velocity-carry
**Field:** **Total consensus: retarget = just write again, from current position, no discontinuity.** Springs carry velocity automatically (continuous physics sim): Svelte, Motion (physics mode), react-spring (`node.lastVelocity` preserved), Reanimated, Solid `createSpring`. The universal caveat: **duration/tween-based animations do NOT carry velocity** — Motion's `duration+bounce` mode, Tween (Svelte/VueUse/Solid), react-spring's `duration` mode all restart position-continuous-but-velocity-discontinuous. VueUse made this an honest *architectural* split (tween in one package, spring in another) precisely because tween can't carry momentum.

**Us:** ✓ **Validated.** Your engine is value+velocity, retarget is `set(x)`, velocity is implicit-and-continuous via Verlet (`current - prevCurrent`). This is the physics-sim continuity everyone converged on. Because you ship spring/decay but **DEFERRED tween/curves**, you sidestep the velocity-discontinuity footgun entirely — you have no leaky position-only primitive yet. When you add tween, heed VueUse's lesson: keep it visibly separate, don't pretend it carries momentum.

### velocity readout
**Field:** **Split, and this is where the field is weakest.** Strong: Motion (`x.getVelocity()` per-second + `useVelocity(x)` returns *another MotionValue* in the graph), react-spring (`get velocity()` live getter). Weak/absent: Svelte (NO public `.velocity` — computed internally, never exposed; gap flagged in its own deep-dive), Solid primitives (none — flagged as a "weak spot"), Reanimated (velocity flows *in* from gestures but no general readout), VueUse, Angular (none).

**Us:** ✓ **Validated, on the right side of the split.** `createVelocity(signal) -> per-second readout of any accessor` is **exactly Motion's `useVelocity`** — velocity as a derived signal, another node in the graph, composable into anything. And `createVelocity` working on *any* accessor (not just springs) is more general than Motion's, which only derives from MotionValues. This directly fixes the #1 named gap in Svelte and Solid. The per-second (framerate-independent) choice matches Motion exactly.

### decay/fling as its own primitive
**Field:** **Divided, and the divide is instructive.** Dedicated primitive: Reanimated `withDecay({velocity, deceleration, clamp})`, Motion `type:"inertia"` (with `power`, `timeConstant`, `modifyTarget` snap, `min`/`max` bounce), react-spring `config.decay` flag. **No decay at all:** Svelte (its own deep-dive calls this "the clearest gap relative to Motion/React-Spring"), Solid primitives ("essentially unsolved"), VueUse (fling is "emergent spring behavior"), Angular (none). The libs that take gestures seriously (Reanimated above all) all have a real decay primitive.

**Us:** ✓ **Validated, and you're with the gesture-serious camp.** `createDecay(initial, { clamp?, snap? }) -> [value, fling]` is a first-class primitive — `clamp`/`snap` map directly onto Reanimated's `clamp` and Motion inertia's `modifyTarget`/`min`/`max`. This is the **single most important place you diverge from the Solid/Svelte lineage you're descended from** and it's clearly correct: you're in a Flutter touch context, so the Svelte/Solid "no fling" gap would have been fatal. `fling(velocity)` to coast and `fling.to(pos)` to drive position during drag is a clean two-method split for the drag→release handoff that none of the others express quite this directly.

### settle (resolve-vs-reject)
**Field:** **The field is genuinely split, and the split is a known footgun.** Resolve-only (interruption is data, not error): react-spring (`{finished, cancelled, noop}` flags, NEVER rejects — "interruption is data, not an exception"), Motion (resolve on settle, cancel routed through a separate *event* channel), VueUse (`transition()` resolves even on abort), Reanimated (`finished===false` callback, no promise). **Reject-on-interrupt:** Svelte 5 (`set()` first line is `this.#deferred?.reject(new Error('Aborted'))` — and they have to attach `.catch(noop)` to suppress the unhandled rejection from the discarded `.target=` path). **Silently strands (the anti-pattern):** Solid `createSpring`'s setter Promise neither resolves nor rejects on retarget — `await setSpring(x)` **hangs forever**. Its own deep-dive flags this as "the sharpest ANTI-pattern to avoid."

**Us:** ✓ **Validated — you threaded the needle correctly.** `set(x) -> Promise<"settled"|"interrupted">`, **ALWAYS resolves, never rejects**. This is react-spring's "interruption is data" wisdom (the resolved value *is* the discriminant: `"settled"` vs `"interrupted"`, mirroring `{finished, cancelled}`) without react-spring's clunky multi-flag object. You explicitly avoid:
- Svelte's reject-on-interrupt (which forces the `.catch(noop)` ceremony and makes fire-and-forget unsafe — your "fire-and-forget safe" note is a direct rejection of this),
- Solid's strand-forever hang (the named worst case),
- Reanimated's callback-only (no awaitable promise).

Resolving the *interrupted* promise (rather than leaving it pending) is precisely the fix Solid's primitive needed. This is one of your strongest decisions.

### derive-many
**Field:** **Consensus: one driver, derive many — and in signal libs it's nearly free.** Motion `useTransform(() => x.get()*2)` (a `createMemo` for animation). react-spring `value.to(fn)` returns another fluid value. Reanimated `useDerivedValue`. Solid: read one animated accessor inside multiple memos/JSX bindings — fine-grained reactivity gives it for free. Svelte: ordinary `$derived(() => f(spring.current))`. The *alternative* (animate a composite object: Svelte `Spring({x,y,scale})`, react-spring's `SpringValues` record) exists but the value-mapping approach dominates.

**Us:** ✓ **Validated, idiomatic.** "One driver, derive many — derive props via plain functions/memos from one spring." This is exactly the Solid-idiomatic answer and matches Motion/react-spring/Reanimated. Your decision to make motion **SCALAR** and compose vectors as N scalar springs (rather than ship generic-T composite animation) is defensible — but see "missing/wrong" below, because the field leans the *other* way on composites.

### stagger / sequence
**Field:** **Split between "rich built-ins" and "userland it."** Rich: react-spring (`useTrail` chains each spring's `to` to the previous controller's fluid value — stagger as reactive *following*, not delay math; plus `useChain`, `trail` prop), Motion (`stagger(0.1)` helper + array-of-segments with `at`/labels/`"<"`), Angular legacy (`query(':enter', [stagger(100, ...)])`, `sequence()`, `group()`). **Userland it:** Svelte (none — await `.set()` promises or `setTimeout`), Solid primitives (none), VueUse (manual `delay`, chain `await`), Reanimated (`withSequence`/`withDelay` — compose, but stagger = per-element `withDelay(index*50)`). **No one has a true general timeline** except via segment arrays.

**Us:** ✓ **Validated.** "stagger = delay-from-`<For>`-index; sequence = await the settle promise. NO timeline primitive." This is *exactly* the Svelte/Solid/VueUse userland approach, and your `await`-the-promise sequence is identical to Svelte/VueUse. Notably, your `delay` option on `set()` and on the constructor is what makes index-based stagger ergonomic — Reanimated's `withDelay(index*50)` model. **One idea you're leaving on the table:** react-spring's `useTrail` "stagger-by-following" (each element springs toward the previous element's *live value*, not a computed delay) — see standouts.

### spring/tween/decay split
**Field:** **Genuinely divided — both models have strong backers.** Separate primitives: Reanimated (`withSpring`/`withTiming`/`withDecay`), Solid (`createTween` vs `createSpring`, different packages/shapes), Svelte (`Spring` vs `Tween` classes). Unified-under-config: Motion (`type: "tween"|"spring"|"inertia"`), react-spring (one `SpringValue`, config regime switches via `duration`/`decay`/tension), solid-motionone (one `transition` object). **No clear winner** — react-spring/Motion argue config-unification reads cleanly at the call site; Reanimated/Svelte argue separate primitives are clearer.

**Us:** ✓ **Validated — and you found a third, better framing.** You have *one engine* (value+velocity+rAF+settle) with **three faces** (`createSpring` target-pull, `createDecay` velocity-coast, `createElapsed` clock) plus `createMotion(target, propagate)` as the custom-physics escape hatch. This is *unified mechanism, separate surfaces* — you get Reanimated/Svelte's clarity (distinct named primitives the user picks) AND react-spring/Motion's shared-internals economy. The `createMotion` + `propagate` escape hatch is something **no other library exposes** — they all hide the integrator. It's a no-magic API (per your stated preference) that nobody else dared ship.

### config style
**Field:** Two dialects, increasingly **both offered on one object**: physics (`stiffness`/`damping`/`mass`) vs perceptual (`duration`/`bounce` or `dampingRatio`). Motion, Reanimated `withSpring`, react-spring (`frequency`+`damping` derived), `@vueuse/motion`/`useSpring`, and Motion One (via solid-motionone) all support BOTH with explicit precedence ("physics overrides duration"). `visualDuration` (Motion, Reanimated, Motion One) decouples perceived speed from the oscillation tail. **Named presets** ("gentle"/"wobbly") exist in react-spring (`config.gentle`) but the field is split — Motion/Reanimated/Svelte ship none, expecting raw numbers.

**Us:** ✓ **Validated.** `createSpring(target, { bounce?, duration?, stiffness?, damping? })` — both dialects on one object, exactly the converged-on dual-mode config (Motion/Reanimated/Motion One). Your "character {bounce,duration} persistent vs transition per-call" bucketing is cleaner than anyone's — it answers a question (which config fields persist across retargets?) that the others leave implicit. **DEFERRING named presets** matches Motion/Reanimated/Svelte (the majority). `spring { wrap: 360 }` for shortest-path rotation is a nice touch most libs lack (Svelte/Motion handle this only via recursive object interpolation, not angle-aware).

## Standout ideas worth stealing

1. **react-spring `useTrail` — stagger-as-following, not delay-math.** Each element's spring `to` is set to the *previous element's live animated value*, so the trail follows by reactive chaining rather than computed per-index delays. Your spec does stagger via `delay-from-<For>-index`, which is fine for entrance but produces *rigid* staggers — every element moves on a fixed offset regardless of interruption. A "follow-the-previous" recipe (trivial in your model: element N's `createSpring` targets `() => elementPrev.value()`) gives staggers that re-coordinate when interrupted mid-flight, and falls straight out of your derived-writable `target` thunk. Worth at least a docs recipe, since it's *free* in your design and visibly better than delay-stagger under interruption.

2. **Motion `x.jump(v)` vs `x.set(v)` — momentum-severing escape hatch.** You have `set(x, { instant })` which severs (good), but Motion makes the distinction a *named verb* on the value: `set` carries momentum, `jump` zeroes velocity + kills animation + ignores effects. Your `instant` option covers the snap case, but consider whether `instant` zeroes velocity (it should — confirm in impl). The naming lesson: "instant set" and "set that carries momentum into the next retarget" are different enough that Motion gave them different names. Your `{ instant }` flag is terser; just make sure the velocity-zeroing semantics are documented as explicitly as Motion's `jump`.

3. **Motion `useVelocity` → another MotionValue you can `useTransform` over.** You have `createVelocity(signal)` already (great), but the *composability* point is the steal: because velocity is just another signal, you can spring-smooth it, threshold it, feed it into a `createDecay` handoff — all in the graph. Make sure a docs example shows `createDecay` being fed `createVelocity(draggedSignal)()` at release. That closes the gesture-handoff loop that Reanimated makes its headline and that Svelte/Solid completely lack.

4. **Reanimated `fling.to(pos)` during drag + `fling(velocity)` at release — you already have this.** Calling it out as *validated-and-rare*: only Reanimated expresses the drag/release split this cleanly (`fling.to` = position-driving during the gesture, `fling` = velocity-coast at release). Keep it. The one addition from Reanimated/Motion inertia: `snap` (you have it) should support a *function* `(target) => snapped` not just a grid, matching Motion's `modifyTarget`.

5. **Motion One / Reanimated `visualDuration` — perceptual duration that ignores the oscillation tail.** Your config has `duration`, but `visualDuration` ("time to *visually appear* to reach target," decoupled from the bouncy settle tail) is the knob designers actually want and is now in three engines (Motion, Reanimated, Motion One). Cheap to add to your `character` bucket; high ergonomic payoff.

6. **VueUse — every config field is a `MaybeRef`.** `useTransition` makes `duration`, `delay`, even `disabled` reactive, so you can *spring the stiffness from a signal*. Your `character` bucket ({bounce, duration}) is persistent-but-presumably-static. Consider letting character params be accessors too — it's the kind of no-magic reactivity that fits Solid and costs nothing.

## What we're missing or got wrong

1. **Composite/vector animation is a real gap, not just a deferral — the field disagrees with "SCALAR only, compose N springs."** Svelte (`tick_spring` recurses over arrays AND object keys), react-spring (`SpringValues` record), Motion (`Spring({x,y,scale})`), VueUse (`useTransition([r,g,b])`) all animate composites with **one physics sim** so the components stay in lockstep. Your "N scalar springs for a vector" approach means each component has its *own* integrator — for `{x, y}` driven by one gesture, the two axes can settle at slightly different times and the velocity-carry isn't shared, producing subtly uncoordinated diagonal motion. Svelte's deep-dive explicitly notes one `Spring({x,y})` drives many properties "in lockstep with a single physics sim." You ship `Color.mix` for color and `wrap:360` for angle — so you've *already conceded* that pure scalar composition isn't enough for every type. I'd flag "vector spring = N independent integrators" as a likely footgun for 2D drag/position and reconsider a `createSpring` that accepts a fixed-shape record sharing one clock, even if you keep the value SCALAR-typed underneath. At minimum, document that N-spring vectors don't share velocity/settle-time.

2. **No reduced-motion answer is shipping while the field increasingly has one.** You've marked it OPEN, but Reanimated ships `reduceMotion` *per-animation* and Motion respects it; this is a WCAG/App Store concern in a Flutter context, not a nicety. The Angular rewrite's whole thesis is "let the platform own motion" partly for accessibility. Recommend resolving the OPEN before shipping animation publicly — a global `reduceMotion` substitution (spring→instant or spring→short-duration) plus the per-`set` override is the converged shape. This is the one OPEN that's a release-blocker, not a v2.

3. **No completion *channel* beyond the per-`set` promise — interruption-as-event is missing.** Motion (`animationComplete`/`animationCancel` via `.on()`), react-spring (`onRest`/`onStart`/`onChange`), Angular (`@trigger.done`), Reanimated (callback) all offer a *standing* completion/lifecycle channel, not just a promise tied to one `set()` call. Your promise resolves per-call, but there's no "fire whenever *this spring* settles, across all retargets" subscription. For things like "play a sound when the sheet finishes settling regardless of how many times the user flung it," the per-`set` promise is awkward (you'd have to re-await each retarget). Consider whether `createElapsed`-style or an `onSettle(spring, cb)` companion is needed. Minor, but the field uniformly has it.

4. **Presence/exit being DEFERRED is the highest-value deferral to revisit first.** Angular's *entire signal-era rewrite* (`animate.leave` + `event.animationComplete()` gating DOM removal, with `MAX_ANIMATION_TIMEOUT` fallback) and solid-motionone's `<Presence exitBeforeEnter>` exist because *unmount* is the one animation case the value-is-a-signal model genuinely cannot express — once the FuseNode is gone, no signal can animate it out. Your `from` handles entrance, but you have **no exit story at all**, and unlike the other deferrals (gestures, scroll, presets are userland-composable from what you have), exit *cannot* be built from `createSpring` alone — it needs renderer cooperation to defer FuseNode disposal until a completion signal fires. The Angular pattern (defer unmount until `animationComplete()`, hard-timeout fallback so a stuck animation can't leak the node) maps directly onto your renderer and is, per your own memory note on Angular's standout, the clearly-stealable idea. I'd promote presence/exit from DEFERRED to "next," because it's the only deferred item that's architecturally load-bearing rather than userland sugar.

5. **`createMotion`'s `propagate` signature returning `void` to mean "settled" is subtle — make sure it's not a footgun.** `(target, prevTarget, current, prevCurrent, dt) => nextValue | void` where `void` = settled is elegant but un-obvious; a user who forgets to return in a branch accidentally settles the animation. Every other library hides the integrator precisely to avoid this. Since you're (correctly, per no-magic preference) exposing it, the docs need a loud warning that *falling off the end* of `propagate` stops the animation — Solid's silent-strand promise is the cautionary tale for "subtle control-flow that does something surprising." Not wrong, but the sharpest edge in the spec.

**Net assessment:** The spec is unusually well-aligned with where the field converged — you matched consensus on value-is-a-signal, retarget-by-write, velocity-carry, derive-many, dual-mode config, and userland stagger, and you landed on the *correct* side of every contested decision (resolve-only settle, real decay primitive, velocity-as-signal, follow-a-reactive-value, no `<View animate>` wrapper). Your genuine improvements over prior art: the derived-*writable* `target` thunk, the `"settled"|"interrupted"` resolve discriminant (fixing Solid's hang and Svelte's reject ceremony at once), the `character`/`transition` config bucketing, and the `createMotion`/`propagate` escape hatch. The two things to fix before public release are **reduced-motion** (release-blocker) and **promoting presence/exit** (architecturally can't be userland'd later). The one decision I'd genuinely re-litigate is **scalar-only vectors** — the whole field shares one sim per composite for a reason.
